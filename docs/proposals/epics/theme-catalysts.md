# Theme Catalysts

## Outcome

An analyst can record a testable catalyst and the thesis claim it matters to on an investment theme. When evidence arrives, the team can see what happened, whether the expected impact followed, and what that means for the thesis.

## How the two systems work together

The **ontology** holds the team's live research graph, including companies, people, themes, relationships, and sourced observations. The **claim graph** holds the team's testable hypotheses, catalysts, and scenario predictions. Claim records link to the existing ontology entities and relationships they concern. The theme page gives analysts a view of both.

## Benefits

The team gets a shared view of what it is watching, why it matters, and what evidence would change its view. Keeping the original expectation alongside later evidence lets the team learn from both accurate calls and missed impacts.

## Phasing

### Phase 1. Establish the claim graph

On the theme page, analysts create and browse the following linked records.

- **Hypothesis.** What the analyst expects to be true about a theme, entity, or relationship, with an author and a horizon.
- **Catalyst.** A testable future event, its expected date or window, and what would count as occurrence.
- **Scenario prediction.** The outcome the analyst expects if a catalyst occurs and the stated assumptions hold. Record how that outcome would bear on the hypothesis, the affected relationship or facet and its current value where applicable, the forecast horizon, and what observation would support or contradict the prediction.

The phase is complete when a teammate can open the theme and trace a hypothesis to its catalyst and conditional prediction, including what would test each. Recording these analyst-authored expectations is the full scope of Phase 1.

### Phase 2. Establish the feedback loop

Link sourced observations from the ontology to the records they resolve, support, or contradict. Review whether the catalyst happened, whether the scenario's predicted impact followed, and what that evidence means for the hypothesis. Each judgment can have a different outcome or date. Keep an item open when the evidence is insufficient, and record timing misses separately from evidence against the expected impact.

Retain the original expectation and a dated history of evidence, decisions, and revisions. The team can reaffirm, revise, or retire the claim and record its reasoning. When the evidence warrants a change to the live ontology, review the sourced diff before applying it through the existing write workflow. [Scenarios & Prediction](scenarios-and-prediction.md) covers proposals for graph changes and forecasts. Corroborate material changes prompted by rumors and count repeated coverage of the same underlying event as one piece of evidence.

## Draft user journey

### Track an earnings call against a theme

1. In Phase 1, an analyst opens the data center rollout theme, links the operator and affected supplier, and records a catalyst. Will the operator announce a deployment delay at its upcoming earnings call?
2. The analyst links the catalyst to the team's hypothesis that the supplier's exposure will stay positive over the next two quarters. They record a scenario prediction that a delay would push enough orders beyond that window to turn exposure mixed. A teammate can see the call date, the announcement that would resolve the catalyst, and the delivery evidence that would test the prediction.
3. In Phase 2, the operator announces a one-quarter delay. The analyst links the earnings release and marks the catalyst as occurred. The supplier impact remains open.
4. Later delivery data shows that orders stayed within the two-quarter window. The analyst links that evidence, records that it contradicts the expected impact, and keeps the supplier's exposure positive. The team can see what the analyst originally expected alongside both outcomes.
